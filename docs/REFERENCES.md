# Research register

Verified against primary sources on 24 October 2026. These are present-day attitudes and self-reported behavior, not forecasts of 2040 or validation of UNSCRIPTED. No primary research participants or product outcome improvements are claimed.

1. **Ping Identity, 2026 Consumer Survey: AI Trust Report.** [Original report](https://www.pingidentity.com/en-us/docs/assets/4423-2026-consumer-survey-ai-trust). Talker Research online double-opt-in survey, 17–25 June 2026; global n=11,000 across 12 countries, India n=500 internet-connected consumers. The report's narrative states 70% in India are willing to independently delegate decisions and access; the country-specific willingness item/base is not separately displayed in the accessible report. Therefore this wording is attributed to the report, not presented as a verbatim survey item. The displayed misrepresentation item asks how concerned respondents are that an agent acting for them could misrepresent their personality or values: India 42% very + 45% somewhat concerned = 87%. No inference of same-person overlap. Global spending and double-checking figures are omitted to keep the case focused.

2. **Pew Research Center, How Americans View AI and Its Impact on People and Society, 17 September 2025.** [Report](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/), [full report PDF, page 16](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/09/PS_2025.9.15_AI-and-its-impact_report.pdf). 5,023 US adults surveyed 9–15 June 2025 through the American Trends Panel, online or phone, weighted to represent US adults. 61% would like more control over **how** AI is used in their lives. On a separate item, 57% report not too much/no control over **whether** AI is used in their lives. These are not India figures or directly comparable to Ping's questions.

3. **Lee, Sarkar, Tankelevitch, Drosos, Rintel, Banks and Wilson, CHI 2025.** [Microsoft Research publication](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713778). Survey of 319 knowledge workers with 936 first-hand GenAI task examples. Higher confidence in GenAI was associated with lower reported critical-thinking effort. Self-reported association does not demonstrate cognitive decline, causal effects, or effectiveness of this product.
